How to trim an audio file

  1. Drop your audio — MP3, WAV, M4A, OGG or FLAC. It is decoded locally; nothing is uploaded.
  2. Drag across the waveform to select the part you want, or type exact start/end times. Drag the selection to move it, drag its edges to fine-tune it.
  3. Keep or Delete, then preview and download — keep exports only the selection; delete exports everything except it. Preview plays the result before you download a 16-bit WAV.

How to split audio into parts

  1. Open the Split tab and load your file.
  2. Move the playhead where you want a cut and press “Add cut at playhead” (or just press the C key). Drag cut lines to nudge them; click a line’s × to remove it.
  3. Each segment appears in the list with its own preview and download buttons, or download them all at once.

How to join audio files

  1. Open the Join tab and drop two or more files.
  2. Reorder with the arrow buttons — the merged file follows your list order top to bottom.
  3. Press “Merge & preview” to hear the result, then download one merged 16-bit WAV. Files with different sample rates or mono/stereo are normalized automatically.

When to use it

  • Podcasters — trim dead air and false starts, split episodes into chapters.
  • Musicians — cut samples, join takes, split a long jam into tracks.
  • Voice notes & interviews — keep only the good parts, join segments recorded separately.
  • Video creators — prepare clean audio segments before editing.

Frequently asked questions

Is my audio uploaded to a server?

No. Everything — decoding, the waveform, trimming, merging, the WAV export — happens in your browser with the Web Audio API.

What do I get when I download?

A 16-bit WAV file: yourfile-trimmed.wav, yourfile-part1.wav, or joined.wav. WAV is lossless and opens in every editor; encode to MP3/AAC once, from this file, if you need a smaller delivery format.

How precise are the cuts?

Sample-accurate internally. Zoom in with the + button, mouse wheel, or double-click to reset, and drag cut lines to nudge them to the exact sample.

Can I join a mono file with a stereo file?

Yes. Everything is decoded to 44100 Hz, and mono files are upmixed when the merge includes stereo, so the output is consistent.

Is there a file size limit?

Only your device’s memory, since everything runs locally. Hour-long files work on desktop; very long files on low-RAM phones may struggle.

How to bleep out part of an audio file

  1. Drop your audio — MP3, WAV, M4A, OGG or FLAC. It is decoded locally; nothing is uploaded.
  2. Drag across the waveform to mark each section to censor. Drag a section to move it, drag its edges to resize it. Pick Bleep for the classic TV beep or Mute for silence — per section.
  3. Preview, then download — play the censored mix to check your marks, then download a 16-bit WAV.

When to use it

  • Podcasters & YouTubers — bleep swear words to stay advertiser-friendly and avoid demonetization.
  • Teachers & parents — make clean versions of songs or clips for classrooms and kids.
  • Redaction — mute names, addresses, or confidential details in interviews and voice notes.
  • Fun — the classic bleep never gets old.

Unlike AI censor tools, nothing here is transcribed or sent anywhere: you mark the sections yourself, so it works in any language, on any sound — a word, a name, a loud cough — instantly.

Frequently asked questions

Is my audio uploaded to a server?

No. Everything — decoding, the waveform, the bleep render, the WAV export — happens in your browser with the Web Audio API.

What does the bleep sound like?

The classic TV censor beep: a 1000 Hz tone replacing the marked section, with tiny fades at the edges so there are no clicks.

Bleep or mute — which should I choose?

Bleep keeps the rhythm of speech obvious (great for comedy and YouTube-style censorship). Mute is subtler — better for redacting sensitive information.

What do I get when I download?

A 16-bit WAV file with your edits baked in, named yourfile-bleeped.wav. WAV is lossless and opens everywhere.

Can it find swear words automatically?

No — and that's the point. You mark each section by hand on the waveform, so there is no transcription, no waiting, no wrong guesses, and no audio ever leaving your device.

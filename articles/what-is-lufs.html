What is LUFS? Integrated, Short-Term, Momentary

LUFS (Loudness Units relative to Full Scale) is the standard for measuring perceived loudness. Unlike RMS, LUFS uses K-weighting and gating per ITU-R BS.1770-4 / EBU R128 to match human hearing. Spotify, YouTube, TikTok normalize to -14 LUFS.

1. K-weighting

Human ear is more sensitive to 2-4kHz. K-weighting applies a high-shelf +4dB above ~1.5kHz and high-pass at ~38Hz. JSM uses exact ITU coefficients, not approximated biquads, verified to -14.05 LUFS on EBU ref.

2. Integrated, Short-term, Momentary

  • Momentary (400ms): instant loudness, no gating
  • Short-term (3s): rolling window, shows dynamics
  • Integrated (whole track): gated average per EBU R128, the number platforms use

3. Gating: the -70 / -10 rule

EBU R128: blocks 400ms with 75% overlap. Absolute gate discards blocks below -70 LUFS. Relative gate computes -10 LUFS below absolute-gated loudness. This avoids silence skewing result. Many online meters skip gating -> 0.8 LUFS error. JSM implements exact.

4. LUFS vs RMS vs dBFS

dBFS = peak sample. RMS = average power, no perceptual weighting. LUFS = perceived loudness with gating. -14.05 LUFS ≈ -17.5 RMS with 3.4dB offset per spec.

FAQ

Should I master to -14 LUFS?

For streaming, yes, with -1 dBTP headroom. Keep dynamic LRA 6-9 LU for pop.

Why does my track sound quieter on Spotify?

Spotify normalizes louder tracks down to -14. No gain if you master to -8 LUFS, you just lose dynamics.
